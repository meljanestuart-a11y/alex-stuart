Biographical Overview (b. 1940)

Sporting excellence meets civic duty.

Alexander Stuart (b. 1940) is a pivotal figure in Scottish sport. Beginning as a young laboratory technician in Aberdeen studying for his Highers, he built his career through patience and discipline. As a player for Dundee F.C. during their most successful era, as Scotland's youngest manager at Montrose, and later as Dundee's Director of Leisure, he oversaw a transformation in how the public engaged with sport and recreation.

Dundee F.C. Era (1958–1969)

From Aberdeen East End to Dens Park

In 1958 Alec Stuart was a young Aberdeen footballer whose life was not yet centred entirely on the game. Working as a laboratory technician with a chemical research firm while playing junior football for Aberdeen East End, he attracted interest from Cardiff City, Swansea, Norwich City, and Wolves. Legend has it he had a railway ticket for Preston in his pocket when Dundee moved to sign him on 29 November 1958.

An Unusual Signing

Manager Willie Thornton was reportedly ill in bed with the flu, so trainer Sammy Kean was sent north carrying the necessary forms. Kean secured Alec’s signature, marking the only player Kean ever signed. The quiet young left-back would eventually become a central figure in one of Dundee’s greatest eras.

Waiting and the Milan Breakthrough (1963)

Alec spent several years in the reserves, learning his trade and transitioning from left-back to left-half under Bob Shankly. When captain Bobby Cox was injured before Dundee’s European Cup semi-final against AC Milan, 22-year-old Alec stepped in for both legs at the San Siro and Dens Park, proving he belonged on Europe's largest stages.

Captaincy and European Nights

By 1967, Alec captained Dundee in the League Cup Final against Celtic. Days later, he scored twice from long range in a 3–1 victory over Royal Liège, earning headlines as the man who shattered the Belgians. He left in April 1969 after 236 competitive appearances and 31 goals, as the last member of the 1961–62 championship squad to depart Dens.

The Highest-Scoring Defender

Alec achieved 31 competitive goals for Dundee. Dens Park supporters would chant "Stuart!" whenever Dundee had a set piece near goal.

Montrose Era (1969–1975)

The Youngest Boss in Scotland

Alec arrived at Links Park in November 1969 as a 29-year-old player-manager. Over six years, while teaching primary school in Dundee during weekdays, he built Montrose into a respected force known for organisation, player development, and memorable cup runs.

Three Roles at Once

Alec balanced teaching in Dundee with training a part-time squad at Links Park. His partnership with former Dundee teammate Kenny Cameron formed a stable backroom team. "The Montrose method" combined discipline and positional sense, helping part-time players compete against full-time opponents.

Hampden Semi-Final (1975)

Following impressive cup scalps—including taking Hearts to a Tynecastle replay in 1970 and defeating Hibernian in 1975—Montrose reached the League Cup semi-final against Rangers at Hampden on 8 October 1975, leading 1–0 at half-time before eventually falling 5–1. Willie Ormond also appointed him assistant manager for a Second Division representative side.

Ayr United & St Johnstone (1975–1980)

The Final Years in the Dugout

Succeeding Ally MacLeod at Ayr United in November 1975, Alec immediately won his first match against Aberdeen, prompting the famous headline: "Alex tries on Ally's shoes—and they fit!" He guided part-time Ayr to a 6th-place finish in the top-flight Premier Division.

Bayern Munich & Global Tours

In May 1976, Bayern Munich used Somerset Park to train before the European Cup Final, leading to a memorable photograph with Uli Hoeneß. That same summer, Ayr toured Nigeria, defeating Enugu Rangers 3–0, and later defeated Newcastle United in the Anglo-Scottish Cup.

Launching Ally McCoist at St Johnstone

Moving to St Johnstone in October 1978, Alec's tenure is immortalized by his trust in youth. During a difficult rebuilding spell, he handed a 16-year-old schoolboy striker his senior debut on 7 April 1979 against Raith Rovers—launching the legendary career of Ally McCoist.

c. 1980–2001 & Beyond

A Life Beyond the Touchline & Retirement

When Alec’s period as a senior football manager concluded in 1980, he transitioned into public service as Dundee District Council’s leisure and recreation manager, eventually serving as Director of Leisure and Parks for Dundee City Council until his official retirement on 20 August 2001.

Global Connections & Major Facilities

Bobby Charlton Soccer Schools & City Infrastructure

In October 1981, Alec persuaded Sir Bobby Charlton to establish his Scottish soccer school in Dundee rather than Edinburgh, creating a recurring sporting fixture that lasted into the 1990s.

He was also instrumental in coordinating major civic projects, including the Dick McTaggart, Lynch, and Douglas sports centres, the refurbishment of the original Olympia swimming complex, the Dundee Ice Arena, and the city marathon.

Post-2001: Still Keen & Striving for Change

Retirement in 2001 did not slow his commitment to sport. Drawing on decades of administrative experience, Alec remained deeply engaged behind the scenes on visionary proposals such as the joint initiative between Dundee F.C., Dundee United, and the University of Abertay to establish a shared £5m youth academy and community sports facility.

Inclusion and International Twinning

Alec pioneered sports opportunities for disabled athletes (such as the Fairmuir Sports Club) and strengthened international friendships through active sports exchanges with Dundee's twin city, Würzburg, Germany.
